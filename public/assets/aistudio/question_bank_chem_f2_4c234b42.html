CHAPTER ONE: ATOMIC STRUCTURE
Exercise 1.1
1. How did the discovery of the nucleus refine earlier atomic models?
2. Why are atoms considered electrically neutral under normal conditions?
3. Compare the Rutherford atomic model with the Bohr atomic model.
4. The atomic number is unique for each element in the periodic table. Explain.
Exercise 1.2
1. Complete the table by filling in the number of protons, electrons, and neutrons for Sodium (Z=11, A=23), Oxygen (Z=8, A=16), Beryllium (Z=4, A=9), and Fluorine (Z=9, A=19).
2. An atom has 10 electrons: (a) Show how these electrons would be arranged in shells. (b) Why is it important to know electron arrangement around the nucleus?
3. Carbon has multiple atomic masses (carbon-12, carbon-13, carbon-14). Why do these forms have different mass numbers and still belong to the same element?
4. How are isotopes identified based on their atomic number and mass number?
5. Provide two ways in which the knowledge of atomic numbers is useful in chemistry.
6. Write the nuclide notation of an atom with 7 protons, 7 neutrons, and 7 electrons and explain its significance.
7. Isotopes of an element are chemically identical but physically different. Explain.
Revision Exercise 1 — Selected Questions
1. What are nucleons? (a) Neutrons and electrons (b) Neutrons and protons (c) Electrons and protons (d) Protons, neutrons and electrons.
2. Which atomic property is the basis for the use of carbon-14 in archaeology for dating ancient objects?
3. Which sub-atomic particles are equal in number to protons in a neutral atom? (a) Electrons (b) Neutrons (c) Electrons of its ion (d) Neutrons of its ion.
4. The atomic number for an element T is 9. What is its electronic configuration? (a) 2:4:3 (b) 2:5:2 (c) 2:7 (d) 2:6:1.
5. In medical diagnosis and treatment, isotopes are used in: (a) detecting malaria parasites (b) using radioactive tracers for imaging and treating cancer (c) treating typhoid infections (d) determining a patient's age.
CHAPTER TWO: PERIODIC CLASSIFICATION
Exercise 2.1
1. Why were the noble gases not included in Mendeleev's periodic table?
2. How does the electronic configuration of nitrogen compare to that of phosphorus?
3. Explain the significance of the Law of Octaves in understanding periodic trends.
4. Mendeleev left gaps in his periodic table. Predict how these gaps demonstrate his understanding of properties of elements.
5. Mendeleev used atomic masses to arrange elements. If you were to use atomic numbers, how would this impact the organisation of the periodic table?
6. How did Mendeleev's and Newlands' works contribute to understanding relationships between physical and chemical properties?
Exercise 2.2
1. Write the electronic configuration and indicate the group and period for Carbon, Phosphorus, Beryllium, Oxygen, Lithium, Fluorine, and Silicon.
2. Argon and sodium are in the same period but behave differently. Use their electronic configurations to justify their differences.
3. How does an element's electronic configuration determine its position in the periodic table? Provide examples.
4. Why do elements in the same group of the periodic table have similar chemical properties?
5. Predict the position of an element with electronic configuration 2:8:3 in the periodic table and justify your answer.
Exercise 2.3
1. What happens to atomic size as you move down a group? Explain, giving reasons.
2. Predict how the densities of alkali metals change as you move down Group I.
3. The reactivity of alkali metals is directly related to their atomic size. Justify.
4. Investigate the use of lithium in rechargeable batteries. How do its properties contribute to this application?
5. How do electronic configurations of Group I elements determine their reactivity?
6. Compare the solubility of Group II hydroxides in water. How does this trend change down the group?
7. Calcium is essential in human bones and teeth. How do its chemical properties contribute to this biological role?
8. Why are Group II elements less reactive than Group I elements? Use electronic configurations to support your answer.
9. Why do reactivities of Group VII elements decrease down the group? Use fluorine and iodine as examples.
10. Explore changes in physical states observed across Group VII elements. What trend is observed within the group?
CHAPTER THREE: CHEMICAL BONDING, FORMULA AND NOMENCLATURE
Exercise 3.1

3. Why is NaCl soluble in water but not in cooking oil, and how does chemical bonding explain this?
Exercise 3.2

2. Explain why ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) is a covalent compound, while ammonium chloride ($\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$) is an ionic compound.

Exercise 3.4

3. A compound contains 52.17% carbon, 13.04% hydrogen, and 34.78% oxygen. Determine its empirical and molecular formulas if RMM is 46.

CHAPTER FOUR: CHEMICAL REACTIONS
Exercise 4.1
1. Balance the following chemical equations:
(a) $\text{Na}(s) + \text{Cl}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{NaCl}(s)$
(b) $\text{P}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{P}_2\text{O}_5(s)$
(c) $\text{Zn}(s) + \text{CaSO}_4(aq) \rightarrow \text{ZnSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$
(d) $\text{C}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g) \rightarrow \text{CO}(g)$
(e) $\text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{HCl}(aq) \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g)$
2. Balance each of the following chemical equations and write its net ionic equation:
(a) $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3(aq) + \text{HCl}(aq) \rightarrow \text{NaCl}(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g)$
(b) $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) + \text{KOH}(aq) \rightarrow \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$
(c) $\text{Ca}(s) + \text{HCl}(aq) \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2(aq) + \text{H}_2(g)$
(d) $\text{Pb(NO}_3)_2(aq) + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) \rightarrow \text{PbSO}_4(s) + \text{NaNO}_3(aq)$
Exercise 4.2
1. Differentiate oxidation from reduction in terms of electron transfer and changes in oxidation states.
2. Write a balanced chemical equation for each of the following reactions:
(a) Zinc reacts with silver nitrate solution to produce zinc nitrate and silver.
(b) Aqueous potassium iodide reacts with aqueous solution of lead(II) nitrate to produce potassium nitrate and lead(II) iodide.
3. Identify the type of reaction in each of the following chemical equations:
(a) $\text{CaO}(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow \text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq)$
(b) $\text{CuCO}_3(s) \rightarrow \text{CuO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)$
(c) $\text{Zn}(s) + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) \rightarrow \text{ZnSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$
(d) $\text{Zn(NO}_3)_2(aq) + 2\text{NaOH}(aq) \rightarrow \text{Zn(OH)}_2(s) + 2\text{NaNO}_3(aq)$
4. A student placed a strip of zinc metal in copper(II) sulfate solution. After a while, the blue colour faded and a reddish-brown deposit formed on the zinc strip. (a) Explain observations and identify reaction type. (b) What would happen if a silver plate replaced zinc in the same solution?
