CHAPTER ONE: THE INTERNAL STRUCTURE OF THE EARTH
Exercise 1.1
1. Describe the difference between the lithosphere and the asthenosphere.
2. Explain why the inner core is solid even though it is the hottest part of the Earth.
3. If the outer core were solid, how would that affect life on Earth?
4. What would happen if the mantle suddenly stopped moving?
5. Why do you think it is essential to understand the Earth's internal layers?
Exercise 1.2
1. Compare and contrast the effects of faulting and folding on the landscape.
2. Explain how internal forces shape the Earth's surface.
3. Explain ways human activities can worsen the Earth's surface.
4. What would your advice be to a community that depends on limestone (sedimentary rock) for making cement, but the mining process affects the environment?
5. Describe economic activities that people engage in relative to the type of rock found in the area. Provide vivid examples in your answers.
Exercise 1.3
1. Explain the concept of vulcanicity and state how it occurs.
2. Compare and contrast between intrusive and extrusive volcanic eruptions.
3. Explain the negative effects of a volcanic eruption on the surrounding community.
4. Explain how volcanic soils can influence agricultural activities.
5. Elaborate on why some people live near volcanoes despite the risks.
6. Describe measures to be taken before, during, and post volcanic incident.
Exercise 1.4
1. Describe the concept of earthquakes and how they occur.
2. Explain the influence of the Earth's crust isostatic adjustment on earthquake occurrences.
3. Explain how earthquakes can disrupt transportation and communication systems in an affected area.
4. Discuss how an earthquake can affect the economy of a country.
5. Describe how earthquake preparedness can reduce damage and save lives.
CHAPTER TWO: THE EXTERNAL STRUCTURE OF THE EARTH
Exercise 2.1

7. Why is the troposphere considered essential for living organisms?

1. What is the economic importance of weathering?

Exercise 2.3

5. How does climate influence the frequency and intensity of mass wasting events?
6. How can mass wasting positively and negatively impact the environment and human activities?
7. How do weathering and mass wasting contribute to shaping the Earth's landscape over time?
Exercise 2.4

2. Explain the processes of river erosion and describe how each process shapes the riverbed and banks.

4. As a farmer, what strategies can you use to prevent soil loss in agricultural areas prone to erosion?
5. Imagine you are an urban planner; what strategies would you use to prevent riverbank erosion while maintaining the natural ecosystem?

7. Why do some rivers entering the ocean not form deltas, and how can human activities influence this process?
8. What key indicators can be used to determine the stage of a river, and how can this information assist in land-use planning?
Exercise 2.5

5. How can urban planners manage rivers effectively to maximize benefits while minimizing negative impacts on human development?
6. How can studying groundwater in limestone regions help local communities sustain water use and prevent environmental hazards like sinkholes and cave collapses?
Exercise 2.6

2. How can communities living in desert areas leverage the benefits of their unique landscapes?
